\section{H2: Lower layers hold syntax, and PIC leaves it intact}
\label{sec:h2}

\paragraph{Hypothesis.}
PIC supervises only a prefix of the layer-$l$ [CLS] vector, $h_l[0{:}d]$, at fixed (layer, width) checkpoints: $(1,16)$, $(2,32)$, $(3,64)$, \dots, $(6,768)$.
The paper motivates this with the finding that ``lower Transformer layers predominantly encode low level linguistic features such as syntax'', while ``higher layers increasingly capture task specific and discriminative signals'' (\S1; \citealp{jawahar-etal-2019-bert,liu-etal-2019-linguistic}).
It also claims that PIC ``allows the remaining dimensions to continue modeling low level and general linguistic features'' (\S1).
The paper measures neither statement, and the cited findings are for BERT-base and ELMo-style models, not for a 6-layer distilled encoder.
We use part-of-speech (POS) tags as the low-level feature and sentiment as the task signal, and test three parts:
\textbf{H2-a}: in untrained TinyBERT, POS information peaks at a lower layer than sentiment information;
\textbf{H2-b}: MIPIC's layers 1--3 keep the POS information that MRL has;
\textbf{H2-c}: at the PIC checkpoints, POS information sits outside the prefix, while MIPIC's sentiment gain sits inside it.

\paragraph{Data.}
\textbf{POS}: Universal Dependencies English EWT, release 2.13 \citep{silveira-etal-2014-gold}: 12,544 / 2,001 / 2,077 train / dev / test sentences (204,577 / 25,149 / 25,094 words) with 17 UPOS tags.
\textbf{Sentiment}: the three-class tweet data on which the variants are evaluated (26,732 train, 3,432 test).
We hold out a stratified 10\% of the training tweets for validation.

\paragraph{Models and probes.}
We probe untrained TinyBERT and the four trained variants (3 seeds each; Section~\ref{sec:setup}), frozen, at every layer $l=0,\dots,6$ ($l{=}0$ is the embedding output).
POS uses the vector of each word's first subword, and sentiment uses the [CLS] vector.
Each probe is a multinomial logistic regression on features standardized with training statistics.
The L2 strength $C\in\{10^{-3},10^{-2},\dots,10\}$ is chosen on the validation data (UD dev, or the held-out tweets), and the chosen probe is scored on the test set.
For each task we probe 13 cells: the \emph{layer profile} (all 768 coordinates, layers 0--6), and the \emph{prefix} $[0{:}d]$ and \emph{suffix} $[d{:}768]$ at $(1,16)$, $(2,32)$ and $(3,64)$.
Sentiment probes are repeated over 3 validation splits and averaged.
In total we fit 676 probes.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Probe score} $S$: POS accuracy on UD test, and sentiment macro-F1 on the test tweets.
  \emph{Intuition:} how much of the task a linear classifier can read from the chosen coordinates.

  \item \textbf{Peak layer}: the layer with the highest score over all 768 coordinates.
  \emph{Intuition:} where in the network the information is easiest to read.

  \item \textbf{Paired gap} $\Delta=S_{\text{model}}-S_{\text{MRL}}$, computed within each training seed, with a 95\% bootstrap CI (1,000 resamples of the test set).
  For POS we resample whole sentences, because the words of one sentence are not independent.
  \emph{Intuition:} what SIA or PIC add to plain MRL, or take away from it, with the training seed held fixed.

  \item \textbf{Noise threshold}
  \[
    T=\max\left(2\sigma_{\text{MRL}},\,0.5\right),
  \]
  where $\sigma_{\text{MRL}}$ is the standard deviation of MRL's score over the 3 seeds.
  \emph{Intuition:} a gap counts only if it is larger than normal run-to-run variation and large enough to matter; 0.5 accuracy points is about 125 of the 25,094 test words.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Decision rules.}
We fixed all rules before we computed any result for a trained model.
\textbf{H2-a} holds if the POS peak layer of untrained TinyBERT is below its sentiment peak layer.
\textbf{H2-b}: at each of layers 1--3, MIPIC \emph{damages} POS if the mean $\Delta$ over seeds is below $-T$ and every seed's CI is below 0; it is \emph{within noise} if $|\text{mean }\Delta|\le T$.
H2-b holds if all three layers are within noise.
\textbf{H2-c} holds at a checkpoint if MIPIC's POS(suffix) $\ge$ POS(prefix) in every seed, and MIPIC$-$MRL sentiment(prefix) has its CI above 0 in every seed.
\textbf{Falsification check}: ``PIC leaves POS intact'' only means something if PIC changes the early layers.
An effect of MRL+PIC$-$MRL needs $|\text{mean }\Delta|>T$ with the CIs of all seeds excluding 0 on the same side.
If none of the 6 full-width cells (layers 1--3, both tasks) shows an effect, the hypothesis fails.

\paragraph{Results.}
Table~\ref{tab:h2} shows the main results.
\textbf{H2-a holds.}
In untrained TinyBERT, POS peaks at layer 4 and sentiment at layer 6, and the POS peak comes first in 97\% of bootstrap resamples.
POS forms a plateau over layers 2--4 (within 0.15 points).
After training, the PIC-trained models keep the order but move both peaks down (POS to layer 2, sentiment to layer 3 or 4), while in MRL both peak at layer 4.
\textbf{H2-b holds.}
At layers 1--3, MIPIC is within 0.34 points of MRL, inside $T{=}0.5$.
The gap at layer 3 is negative in every seed, but it is below the threshold.
\textbf{The falsification check is met, so H2 fails by our rules.}
MRL+PIC shows no effect at layers 1--3 in either task: its POS gaps are within $T$, and its two sentiment gaps beyond $T$ have CIs that include 0 in most seeds.
PIC hardly changes the full-width early layers, so H2-b says little: there was almost nothing for PIC to damage there.
PIC does act elsewhere.
Past layer 3, the PIC-trained models lose both tasks: MIPIC is 0.7--2.2 POS points and 1.6--2.6 sentiment points below MRL at layers 4--6, with all 18 CIs below 0, and MRL+PIC shows a similar pattern.
\textbf{H2-c holds at 1 of 3 checkpoints.}
The POS part holds everywhere, but it also holds for untrained TinyBERT and MRL, because the suffix has 704--752 coordinates and the prefix only 16--64.
The sentiment gain holds at $(2,32)$ in every seed (+4.6 to +5.9).
It fails at $(1,16)$, where it is +13.5 and +5.1 in two seeds but absent in the third, and at $(3,64)$, where no seed gains.
At $(3,64)$, MIPIC's prefix also holds 5.2 POS points less than MRL's, with every CI below 0.

\paragraph{Takeaway.}
Syntax does peak before the task signal in TinyBERT, as the motivation for PIC says.
But PIC does not so much preserve the early layers as leave them almost unchanged.
Its real effects are a trade inside the small supervised prefix (more sentiment at $(2,32)$, less POS at $(3,64)$) and a loss of both POS and sentiment in layers 4--6.
